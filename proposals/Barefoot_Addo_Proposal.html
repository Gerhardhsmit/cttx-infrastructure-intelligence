01

Where things stand today

The lodge currently pays R18,105.60 a month for a shared, best-effort connection and a set of rented Wi-Fi access points. Nothing about that setup belongs to the lodge, and none of it scales without another monthly line item.

What we found on site

The backhaul radios that carry the whole property's connectivity are mounted on a leaning gum pole — not an engineered structure. It has held so far. It is not something to plan a lodge's connectivity around.

A gap in coverage

The spa currently has no connectivity at all. It was never part of the original rollout, and extending the current rented setup to reach it would mean yet another monthly access-point fee stacked on top of an already contended network.

02

What we're building instead

A private wireless network on an engineered 9m mast, replacing the gum pole entirely. Two sector radios cover the full property from that single mast — the main lodge cluster at close range, and a second sector reaching out to the villas, the staff village, and the Director's House.

Twenty-eight access points go in across every building that needs coverage. That includes the spa — connected for the first time — alongside the boma, the school, the workshop, and every guest and staff area on the property.

The order of work matters. The private network — mast, cabinet, power, radios and access points — is built first. The Vodacom link is the final piece, and it can only go in once the mast is standing: Vodacom surveys the site against that mast before they will install.

Built for what comes next

Because the sector coverage already reaches every corner of the property, adding CCTV later doesn't mean building new wireless infrastructure — it means plugging cameras into a network that's already live there. Security cameras at the boma, the villas, or the staff village become an endpoint cost, not an infrastructure project.

Not a local fix

CTTX is based in Port Elizabeth, but this build runs on Vodacom's national carrier network — over 14,000 towers across South Africa. If there's a second property, or a third, anywhere in the country, CTTX delivers and supports the same carrier-grade design there too. This isn't a one-off local install; it's a model that travels with the properties you own.

03

Why this has to work every time

A lodge network carries three different loads at once. This design was built to hold all three, not just the one that's easiest to sell.

Client Experience

Many guests are international, checking in with family across several time zones — sharing the day's sighting, confirming they've arrived safely, staying reachable somewhere their own cellphone has no signal. That only works if the connection is as reliable at 2am as it is at 2pm.

Operational Requirement

Bookings, POS, staff communication and day-to-day lodge management all run over this same network. A dropped connection on a busy weekend isn't an inconvenience — it's a stalled payment or a missed booking.

Security Requirement

Reserves deal with real incidents — a boundary breach, a security concern that surfaces after the office has closed for the night. When that happens, communication is what determines how fast a response reaches the right people. A network that goes quiet after hours is an operational risk, not a technicality.

99.9%
Designed availability

Built on an engineered mast in place of the gum pole it replaces, a Vodacom Business SLA link already confirmed for high availability at this site, and solar/battery power with no single point of failure. CTTX also monitors the network remotely, so an issue is caught before it becomes an outage.

How to read this: the CTTX line starts at the R358,599 paid up front (the R355,000 build plus the R3,599 connection fee). From month 1 both paths pay their monthly bills — R18,106 to the current provider, R8,949 on the CTTX path. The two lines cross at 2 years 10 months. By the end of the 36-month agreement the lodge has paid R680,763 on the CTTX path against R705,336 to continue renting — R24,573 less — and owns the mast, radios, Wi-Fi and power system outright. Over five years the difference is R352,621.

Assumptions, stated plainly: CTTX's monthly fee is fixed for the 36-month term and assumed to rise 8% a year after it. The current provider's bill is assumed to rise 8% a year from year two (a typical CPI-linked telecom escalation; not confirmed by them). With no escalation on either side, payback is 3 years 4 months. The full year-by-year workings are available as a spreadsheet on request.

Next steps

  • Confirm the design
    Line-of-sight for all 28 connection points is already confirmed by field survey. A final walk-through validates cable runs and exact mounting positions.
  • Approve the investment
    Sign off on the R355,000 build and the 36-month connectivity agreement. The Vodacom feasibility submission goes in at the same time, so it runs in parallel with the build.
  • Build the private network
    Mast, cabinet, power system, radios and access points go in first. This is the part CTTX builds and owns end to end, and it does not wait on Vodacom.
  • Vodacom site survey
    Once the mast is standing, CTTX confirms the site is ready and Vodacom surveys against it. Their installation depends on being satisfied with that mast — which is why it has to be built first.
  • Carrier link installed
    The last piece: the 100 Mbps symmetrical, uncapped, unshaped, unthrottled SLA link from Vodacom's Olifants Nek high site, installed in one go.
  • Go live
    The current connection is decommissioned once the new network is tested and confirmed — including the spa, connected for the first time.
A note on timing

We have secured the wireless equipment for this build with our supplier. This kind of kit tightens up at this time of year, and we can't hold an allocation indefinitely — if it is sold on and the build stands over to next year, it will cost more. That is not a reason to decide in a hurry; it is simply the honest position on where the stock sits today.
